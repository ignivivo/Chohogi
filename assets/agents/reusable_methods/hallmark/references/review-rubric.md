# Hallmark review rubric

Use the applicable checks as a structured manual review. Record observation, impact, and proposed revision; do not emit a pass claim for checks you did not inspect.

## Task and hierarchy

- Is the primary user task visible before decorative or secondary material?
- Does visual weight distinguish primary, supporting, and optional information?
- Does the chosen composition fit the content rather than reuse an interchangeable marketing template?
- Does progressive disclosure reduce noise without hiding necessary action or context?

## Evidence and system fit

- Are metrics, endorsements, logos, screenshots, and claims user-supplied, attributable, or visibly labelled as pending?
- Do type, color, spacing, radius, borders, and shadows use the existing system or document an intentional extension?
- Are images and diagrams informative, accessible, and specific to the task rather than ornamental filler?
- Is interface chrome authentic to the product, rather than decorative fake browser, device, IDE, or terminal framing?

## Interaction and reading comfort

- Are labels, controls, focus states, loading, errors, success, and reduced-motion behavior clear where they apply?
- Does motion reveal state or preserve orientation instead of competing with reading?
- Does density support scanning and task completion at narrow and wide viewports?
- Are emphasis, contrast, and typography serving the content rather than repeating a generic visual trope?

## Handoff

Name the changed semantic and interactive surfaces for accessibility review. Name media, fonts, motion, rendering, and above-the-fold risks for performance review. Name changed client requests or result ownership for async-state review.
